Research

My research sits at the boundary between number theory and combinatorics, with a particular interest in problems where analytic and algebraic tools combine to reveal structure in seemingly chaotic objects.

Analytic number theory

I work on the distribution of prime numbers and on properties of $L$-functions associated with Dirichlet characters. A central object in this line of work is the Riemann zeta function,

$$\zeta(s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^s}, \qquad \operatorname{Re}(s) > 1,$$

and its generalizations, which make it possible to study the distribution of primes in arithmetic progressions via sieve methods and exponential sum estimates.

Additive combinatorics

I study the structure of sumsets $A + B$ for finite subsets of abelian groups, in particular Freiman-type results: if $|A + A|$ is small relative to $|A|$, then $A$ must be contained in a generalized arithmetic progression of bounded dimension and size. These ideas connect with counting problems for arithmetic progressions inside dense sets.

Discrete geometry

I am also interested in incidence problems between points and lines (or curves) in finite and Euclidean spaces, such as the Erdős distinct distances problem, and their connections with additive combinatorics via polynomial methods.
